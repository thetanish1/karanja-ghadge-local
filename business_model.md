# Business Model --- Karanjaghadge Local

## 1. Business overview

Karanjaghadge Local is a hyperlocal, multi-vendor marketplace connecting
residents with independent local shops. It provides digital discovery,
product catalogues, order placement, order coordination and optional
delivery support.

The platform is an intermediary/marketplace. In the initial model, each
order is placed with one shop only. The shop confirms availability and
fulfills the order either through its own delivery, customer pickup, or
a delivery partner coordinated by the platform.

## 2. Participants

### Customers

-   Discover nearby shops.
-   Search products and compare available local options.
-   Place an order from one shop.
-   Select delivery or pickup.
-   Pay through an enabled payment method.
-   Receive order updates and contact support.

### Shopkeepers

-   Apply to list their shop.
-   Add and update products, prices and availability.
-   Accept or reject incoming orders.
-   Prepare orders and hand them over for delivery or pickup.
-   Receive order details and basic sales summaries.

### Delivery partners

-   Receive manually or automatically assigned deliveries.
-   Pick up accepted orders.
-   Update delivery status.
-   Confirm successful delivery.
-   Record COD collection if enabled.

### Platform administrator

-   Verify shops and delivery partners.
-   Manage service areas, categories, users and orders.
-   Handle complaints and cancellations.
-   Configure fees and future monetization.
-   Monitor platform performance.

## 4. Marketplace workflow

1.  Shopkeeper submits a shop application.
2.  Admin verifies and approves the shop.
3.  Shopkeeper adds products, prices, units and availability.
4.  Customer selects a serviceable address.
5.  Customer browses shops and products.
6.  Customer places a single-shop order.
7.  Shopkeeper confirms or rejects the order.
8.  If accepted, the shop prepares the order.
9.  Order is picked up by the customer or assigned delivery partner.
10. Customer receives the order and the order is marked complete.
11. Platform records the order, payment and any applicable fees.

## 5. Initial business model: free pilot

For the launch period: - Shop registration: free. - Shop listing:
free. - Product listing: free. - Order receiving: free. - Platform
commission: 0%. - Shop subscription: ₹0. - Featured placement: free or
disabled. - Customer platform fee: ₹0.

The purpose of the free period is to onboard shops, build a catalogue,
understand customer behavior and validate repeat demand.

Delivery charges, if any, must be shown clearly to customers and paid to
the party providing delivery. A delivery fee is not automatically
platform revenue.

## 6. Future revenue streams

Only introduce monetization after shops and customers receive measurable
value.

### A. Order commission

A percentage of eligible completed orders, agreed with each shop. A
possible test range is 3%--8%, but the final rate must be based on local
economics and shopkeeper acceptance.

### B. Shop subscriptions

Optional monthly plans for enhanced tools, analytics, catalogue support
or promotional features. Basic listing should remain available under a
clearly defined free tier if commercially viable.

### C. Featured shop placement

Optional paid promotion, clearly labelled as sponsored. Do not let paid
placement mislead customers about relevance or availability.

### D. Delivery coordination margin

If the platform arranges delivery, it may charge a transparent
coordination fee only if the delivery economics allow it and the
customer/shop agrees.

### E. Customer membership

A future optional membership could offer benefits such as reduced
delivery charges or member offers. Do not launch this until repeat order
patterns are understood.

## 7. Illustrative unit economics

Example only: - Average order value: ₹250. - Hypothetical commission: 5%
= ₹12.50. - Hypothetical customer delivery charge: ₹20. - Delivery
partner payout: ₹15. - Other variable cost/payment expense: ₹2.

Potential platform contribution per order: - Commission + delivery
charge − delivery payout − other variable cost - ₹12.50 + ₹20 − ₹15 − ₹2
= ₹15.50.

This is not a guaranteed margin. If the delivery fee is passed fully to
the delivery partner, or the platform does not collect it, the
calculation changes. Confirm payment-gateway charges, refunds, taxes and
local delivery costs before setting prices.

At 10 completed orders/day for 30 days, this example would produce
₹4,650 contribution before fixed costs and founder labour (300 ×
₹15.50). Actual results may be lower or negative.

## 8. Operating model

### Pilot

-   One small service area.
-   5--10 participating shops.
-   Manual shop verification.
-   One-shop-per-order checkout.
-   Shopkeeper delivery, customer pickup or manual delivery
    coordination.
-   WhatsApp and phone support.
-   Admin manually monitors order flow.

### Growth

-   More shops and product categories.
-   Dedicated delivery partner onboarding.
-   Improved dispatch and delivery status.
-   Better customer support processes.
-   Repeat-order and shop performance analytics.

### Expansion

-   Nearby villages only after service quality is stable.
-   More delivery capacity.
-   Optional subscriptions and commission.
-   Stronger operational, legal and customer-support processes.

## 9. Key costs

-   Hosting, database and domain.
-   Maps/geocoding API usage.
-   WhatsApp Business messaging/provider charges.
-   Payment gateway fees.
-   Delivery partner payouts.
-   Customer support and refunds.
-   Marketing and printed material.
-   Compliance, accounting and taxes where applicable.

Use free tiers for development where appropriate, but verify usage
limits and production suitability.

## 10. Key risks and mitigations

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Risk                                Mitigation
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Incorrect product availability      Shop confirms before order
                                      acceptance

  Price mismatch                      Show last-updated time; shop
                                      confirms final price

  Low order volume                    Validate demand before spending

  Expensive delivery                  Small service radius and
                                      transparent fee

  Shopkeeper disengagement            Simple onboarding and free pilot

  Cancellations                       Clear order state and cancellation
                                      policy

  Customer trust                      Verified shops, support number and
                                      clear policies

  Payment disputes                    Server-side gateway verification
                                      and transaction records

  Regulatory issues                   Review applicable local, consumer,
                                      tax, food and drug rules
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Key performance indicators

-   Approved and active shops.
-   Catalogue completeness.
-   Monthly active customers.
-   Orders placed, accepted and completed.
-   Repeat purchase rate.
-   Average order value.
-   Out-of-stock rate.
-   Cancellation rate.
-   Average fulfillment time.
-   Delivery cost per completed order.
-   Contribution margin per completed order.
-   Shop retention and customer satisfaction.

## 12. Business principle

Build trust and repeat usage before monetization. The platform should
make local shops easier to discover and order from, while keeping
delivery and customer service reliable. Scale only when the economics
work.
