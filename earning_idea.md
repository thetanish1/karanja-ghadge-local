# Earning Idea --- Karanjaghadge Local

## The idea

Build a hyperlocal marketplace that helps people in Karanjaghadge
discover and order from nearby independent shops. The platform brings
local grocery stores, vegetable sellers, dairies, bakeries, general
stores and other eligible retailers into one digital catalogue.

Customers browse local shops and products, place an order, and choose
delivery or pickup. Shops receive orders directly and confirm
availability. Delivery can initially be handled by the shopkeeper, the
founder, or a manually coordinated local delivery partner.

This is a local-commerce business, not a warehouse-based quick-commerce
operation. The platform should not purchase and hold inventory in the
beginning.

## The customer problem

-   Residents may need to call or physically visit multiple shops to
    find products.
-   Small shops may not have their own website or ordering system.
-   Shopkeepers may want additional orders without building a digital
    storefront.
-   Customers want an easy way to discover nearby shops and request home
    delivery.

Validate these problems through conversations with residents and
shopkeepers before assuming demand.

## The proposed solution

A mobile-first website/PWA or app with: - Local shop discovery. -
Product catalogues and search. - Shop-specific cart and checkout. -
Order requests and shop confirmation. - WhatsApp notifications. -
Delivery or pickup selection. - Google Maps/location support. - UPI/COD
options when safely configured. - Order status updates.

## Why start locally?

A small service area makes it easier to: - Onboard shops in person. -
Verify shop details. - Test delivery distances and costs. - Resolve
order problems quickly. - Build trust through local relationships. -
Learn what customers actually order.

Start only in Karanjaghadge. Expand to nearby villages after repeat
demand and reliable fulfillment are demonstrated.

## Initial target

The first milestone is not a large revenue number. It is proof that the
service is useful.

Suggested first 30-day validation targets: - Speak with 20
shopkeepers. - Obtain permission from 5 shops to participate in a free
pilot. - Interview 20--30 potential customers. - Publish a small
catalogue of real products and prices. - Complete 20--30 genuine
test/pilot orders if demand exists. - Measure order completion, repeat
use, delivery time, cancellations and customer feedback.

These are goals, not guaranteed outcomes.

## What not to do initially

-   Do not buy inventory or operate a dark store.
-   Do not promise 10-minute delivery.
-   Do not hire a full-time delivery team before order volume supports
    it.
-   Do not charge shops before showing them value.
-   Do not build complex multi-shop checkout or automated dispatch
    first.
-   Do not list regulated products without understanding applicable
    laws.
-   Do not claim live tracking if only manual status updates are
    available.

## Success measures

Track: - Number of active shops. - Number of active products. - Customer
sign-ups. - Orders placed and completed. - Repeat customers. - Average
order value. - Cancellation and out-of-stock rates. - Delivery cost per
order. - Contribution margin per order. - Shopkeeper satisfaction.

The business is worth expanding when customers repeat orders, shops keep
their catalogues updated, and each fulfilled order has a sustainable
contribution margin.
